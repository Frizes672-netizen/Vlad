# Short "Struck by Lightning 7 Times" (~1:00, US YouTube)

## Voiceover (paste into ElevenLabs as is — numbers are spelled out on purpose)

This man was struck by lightning seven times. And he survived every single one.

His name was Roy Sullivan. A park ranger in Virginia.

Strike one. Nineteen forty-two. He's in a fire lookout tower. The lightning blows the nail right off his big toe.

Strike two. He's driving his truck. His eyebrows are burned off.

Strike three. He's standing in his own yard. His shoulder is scorched.

Strike four. His hair catches fire. He has to dump a bucket of water on his own head.

Strike five. He swears he saw the cloud… following him.

After that, people stopped standing next to him in storms. Even his coworkers.

Strike six. His ankle.

Strike seven. He's out fishing. Lightning hits him again — and then a bear tries to steal his fish. He fights it off… with a stick.

The odds of this? About one in twenty-two septillion.

Two of his burned ranger hats are still in a museum.

Would you stand next to him in a storm?

## Facts (verify before posting)
Roy C. Sullivan (1912–1983), ranger in Shenandoah National Park, Virginia; Guinness record — struck by lightning 7 times between 1942 and 1977: 1942 lookout tower (lost big toenail), 1969 in his truck (eyebrows burned), 1970 in his yard (left shoulder seared), 1972 ranger station (hair set on fire), 1973 (hair on fire again; said a cloud followed him), 1976 (ankle), 1977 while fishing (chest/stomach burns); after the 7th strike a bear tried to take his trout and he hit it with a branch (his own account). Odds often quoted ≈ 1 in 22 septillion; two of his hats were displayed at Guinness World Exhibit halls.
