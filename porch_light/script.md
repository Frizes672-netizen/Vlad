# Short "The Porch Light" (~2 min, English audience)

## Voiceover (paste into ElevenLabs as is — numbers are spelled out on purpose)

For thirty-one years, my neighbor turned on her porch light at exactly nine-fourteen every single night. Last Tuesday, she died. And at nine-fourteen… the light turned on anyway.

Her name was Mrs. Hale. She lived alone in the white house across the street. Storms, blackouts, Christmas — it didn't matter. At nine-fourteen, the light came on. As kids, we used to set our watches by it.

And every night, at that exact moment, my dad would stop whatever he was doing… and look out our front window.

After the funeral, I helped clean out her house. The porch switch was taped in the "on" position. Next to it, a note in shaky handwriting: "Never turn it off."

In her bedroom, I found out why. A faded missing poster. Daniel Hale. Nine years old. Last seen October third, nineteen ninety-three, walking home from a friend's house. Time: nine-fourteen p.m.

He was never found.

But that night, the bulb was still warm. There was no timer. Someone had been inside.

So the next evening, I waited in the dark. Nine-twelve. Nine-thirteen. Footsteps. A man walked up her driveway, took out a key, and switched on the light. Then he sat down on her steps and covered his face.

I walked over. The man looked up.

It was my father.

He didn't even look surprised. He just said: "Danny was walking home from our house that night. I was supposed to walk him to the corner. I didn't."

He was nine too.

"She found out years later. She never told anyone. She asked me for one thing: the light comes on every night. So I never forget."

Then he handed me the key.

"When I'm gone… it's your turn."

Mrs. Hale never sat facing the street. Her chair faced our window.

Would you keep that promise?
